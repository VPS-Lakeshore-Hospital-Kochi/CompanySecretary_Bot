# What a Company Secretary and Legal team do in an Indian hospital company

This is the map the CS Assistant was built from. It covers the work of the Company Secretary (CS) and the in-house legal team of a large private multi-speciality hospital company in Kerala with foreign shareholding (as at September 2026). For each area it notes which part of the tool helps.

**Screens:** Ask = Ask a question · Check = Check a document · Write = Write a document · Calendar = Filing calendar · Events = Something happened? · Licences = Licences & renewals · Share = Can we share this? · Committees = Committees & decisions · Library = Document library (every record listed below can be kept there, in its own folder, and the assistant can read it)

---

## 1. Board, committees and general meetings

| Work | Law / standard | Tool |

| Plan the year's Board meetings: at least 4, gap of 120 days or less | Sec. 173, SS-1 | Calendar |
| Notice (at least 7 days), agenda, notes on agenda, draft resolutions | Sec. 173(3), SS-1 | Write |
| Meetings by video conference: rules, attendance, recording | Rule 3, Meetings of Board Rules | Ask |
| Resolutions by circulation | Sec. 175 | Write |
| Minutes: draft within 15 days, sign within 30 days, keep the minute books | Sec. 118, SS-1 | Write (turns rough notes into minutes) |

## 2. Filings with the Registrar of Companies (MCA)

**Every year:** AOC-4 (and CFS), MGT-7 with MGT-8, ADT-1 (in an appointment year), DPT-3, MSME-1 (twice), PAS-6 (twice, unlisted public companies), DIR-3 KYC, CRA-2 and CRA-4 (cost audit), CSR-2. All of these are in the **Calendar**, with dates worked out from the AGM date.

**When something happens:** DIR-12 (director or KMP change), MGT-14 (special resolutions and some Board resolutions), CHG-1 / CHG-4 (charges), PAS-3 (allotment), BEN-2 (beneficial owners), INC-22 (registered office), ADT-3 (auditor resignation), and replies to RoC notices, compounding (Sec. 441) and adjudication (Sec. 454). These are in **Events**.

## 3. Shares, investors and foreign investment

- Allotment, transfer, transmission; unlisted public company shares must be in demat form (Rule 9A); share capital reconciliation (PAS-6).
- Foreign investment by the group parent: hospitals allow 100% FDI under the automatic route. FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment, FC-TRS within 60 days of a transfer, annual FLA return by 15 July, downstream investment rules, pricing guidelines. (Calendar, Events, Ask)
- Dividends, unpaid dividend accounts, IEPF transfers.

## 4. Governance and approvals

- Related party transactions with group hospitals, the foreign parent, directors and their relatives: Sec. 188 approvals, Audit Committee (Sec. 177), AOC-2 disclosure. (Events, Ask)
- Loans, guarantees and investments (Sec. 185, 186); borrowing limits and security (Sec. 180).
- CSR: policy, committee, annual action plan, Unspent CSR Account, CSR-2. Health camps and free treatment are common hospital CSR projects; check what qualifies under Schedule VII.
- Delegation of powers and authorised signatories; powers of attorney. (Write)
- Secretarial audit (Sec. 204), internal audit (Sec. 138), cost records and cost audit (Sec. 148; healthcare services are covered above the turnover limits).
- Vigil mechanism / whistle-blower policy, code of conduct, D&O insurance.

## 5. Hospital licences and regulators

A hospital holds more licences than most companies. Missing one can shut a department down. All of these are pre-loaded in **Licences** so their expiry dates can be tracked, and new services are covered in **Events**.

| Area | Law | What to keep in order |
|---|---|---|
| Registration of the hospital | Kerala Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act 2018 | Permanent registration, display of rates, minimum standards |
| Ultrasound / sex-determination ban | PC&PNDT Act 1994 and Rules | Registration of each machine and place, Form F, monthly report by the 5th, 30-day notice of changes |
| Radiation (X-ray, CT, cath lab, radiotherapy, nuclear medicine) | Atomic Energy (Radiation Protection) Rules 2004 | AERB licences on eLORA, Radiation Safety Officer, dosimetry |
| Organ transplant | THOTA 1994, Rules 2014 | Registration (5 years), Authorisation Committee, K-SOTTO |
| Blood centre, pharmacy, narcotics | Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940, NDPS Act 1985 | Blood centre licence, retail drug licences, Recognised Medical Institution |
| Clinical trials and research | New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules 2019, ICMR guidelines | Ethics Committee registration, clinical trial agreements, compensation |
| IVF / surrogacy / MTP | ART Act 2021, Surrogacy Act 2021, MTP Act | Registration, approval of place |
| Mental health | Mental Healthcare Act 2017 | Registration with the State Mental Health Authority |
| Waste and pollution | Bio-Medical Waste Rules 2016; Water and Air Acts | BMW authorisation, annual report by 30 June, consent to operate |
| Building safety | Fire, lifts, boilers, electrical, legal metrology | NOCs, annual licences and stamping |
| Accreditation and payers | NABH, NABL; CGHS, ECHS, KASP / PM-JAY, insurers and TPAs | Accreditation cycles, empanelment renewals |

## 6. Contracts: drafting and checking

The legal team's biggest daily workload. **Write** has templates for each of these, and **Check** has a checklist for each:

- **NDAs.** Vendors, IT, M&A and JV discussions, research. Watch for one-sided definitions, foreign law, patient data shared under an NDA instead of a data processing agreement, and non-compete clauses (generally void after the contract ends under Sec. 27 of the Contract Act).
- **Consultant and visiting doctor agreements.** Keep it a professional relationship, not employment. Check NMC / State Medical Council registration, professional indemnity cover, no fee-splitting, TDS as professional fees, and who owns the medical records.
- **Vendor, service and outsourcing agreements** (housekeeping, security, catering, laundry, IT). Check SLAs, the principal employer's liability for contract labour under the Labour Codes, data access and caps on liability.
- **Equipment purchase with CMC / AMC.** Check uptime guarantees, spares for the life of the equipment, AERB and CDSCO compliance, and cybersecurity.
- **Leases and leave-and-licence.** A lease for more than 11 months must be registered. Check stamp duty under the Kerala Stamp Act, lock-in and permitted use.
- **MoUs** with nursing and medical colleges, **clinical trial agreements**, **insurer / TPA / corporate empanelments**, **data processing agreements** (DPDP Act), and **referral and medical-travel facilitator agreements**.
- Before execution: stamp duty, registration, authority to sign, Board or shareholder approval (related party, Sec. 186, Sec. 180), and any regulatory approval.

## 7. Disputes and legal responses

- **Legal notices**, often alleging medical negligence. Note the reply date and tell the indemnity insurer at once. Secure the medical records, get statements from the doctors, and reply without admitting liability. Patient records must be supplied within 72 hours of a request. (Events, Check, Write)
- **Consumer Commission cases.** The written version is due within 30 days, extendable by 15 days only (Consumer Protection Act 2019, Sec. 38). Courts judge negligence by the tests in Jacob Mathew (2005) and the Bolam standard. Informed consent follows Samira Kohli (2008).
- **Criminal exposure.** Cite the new criminal laws for events after 1 July 2024: BNS Sec. 106 (negligent death), BNSS for procedure, and BSA Sec. 63 for electronic evidence. Medico-legal cases, POCSO reporting and police intimation also sit here.
- **Recovery of dues** from corporates and insurers: legal notice, pre-institution mediation under Sec. 12A of the Commercial Courts Act, arbitration, and Sec. 138 NI Act for bounced cheques.
- **Regulator notices** from the RoC, RBI, tax, PCPNDT, KSPCB or drugs authorities. (Check analyses the notice; Write drafts the reply.)
- **Labour disputes** under the Industrial Relations Code 2020, and landlord or property disputes.

## 8. Data protection and cyber security

- DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025 (phased in). The hospital is a Data Fiduciary for patient and staff data. It needs clear notices and consent, purpose limitation, security safeguards, contracts with processors (HIS, lab and cloud vendors), breach reporting to the Data Protection Board and to patients, and a grievance officer.
- CERT-In Directions 2022: report cyber incidents within 6 hours and keep logs for 180 days.
- Events has a "Patient data leak, cyber attack" checklist, and Write drafts the privacy notice and the DPA.

## 9. Employment-side compliance the CS is usually asked about

- The four Labour Codes came into force on 21 November 2025. They replace 29 older laws and change registers, returns and contract-labour rules.
- POSH Act: an Internal Committee, an annual report to the District Officer, and the complaint numbers in the Board's report.
- Doctors' and nurses' registrations; the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act equal opportunity policy.

## 10. Hospital committees

The CS office usually services, or keeps the records of, committees that hospital law requires as well as the Board's own committees. **Committees** lists them with their members, last meeting, the next meeting due and the reconstitution or registration date.

| Committee | Law | What the CS watches |
|---|---|---|
| Transplant Authorisation Committee | THOTA 1994, Sec. 9; THOTA Rules 2014 | Government order constituting it and when it expires; every living-donor file complete for the kind of donor; separate interviews; video recording; decision with reasons, displayed and reported to the Appropriate Authority / K-SOTTO; right of appeal. **Committees > Transplant files** holds the document checklist for each file. **Check** reviews a file, and **Write** drafts the minutes and decision. |
| Institutional Ethics Committee | NDCT Rules 2019; ICMR Guidelines 2017 | CDSCO and DHR registrations and their renewal; composition; records kept at least 5 years after a trial ends |
| POSH Internal Committee | POSH Act 2013, Sec. 4, 21 | Composition (presiding woman officer, external member), 3-year tenure, annual report |
| NABH committees (infection control, quality, pharmacy & therapeutics, medical records) | NABH standards | Meeting frequency and minutes for accreditation |

## 11. Records: who may see them, and how long to keep them

Requests for records arrive every week. **Share** gives a first answer for each kind of record and each kind of requester, keeps a **Requests register**, and shows a **retention schedule**.

- **Company records.** Members may inspect the statutory registers and the minutes of general meetings (Sec. 94, 119), but not Board minutes. Directors may see the books of account (Sec. 128(3)). Auditors have access to all records (Sec. 143). The RoC can inspect under Sec. 206 and 207. Lenders get certified extracts of the resolutions they need, not the minutes. A buyer's or investor's due diligence happens under an NDA, in a data room, with approval.
- **Patient records.** The patient gets copies within 72 hours (IMC Regulations 2002, Reg. 1.3.2). Anyone else needs the patient's consent, a written police requisition, or a summons or order (BNSS Sec. 94). Special laws protect HIV status, MTP, POCSO, mental health, ART and transplant files. The media and the public get nothing.
- **People records.** Employees can see their own data (DPDP Act Sec. 11). POSH inquiry records must never be made public (Sec. 16).
- **RTI** does not apply to a private hospital company.
- **Retention.** Minutes and the main registers are kept permanently. Books of account: 8 years (Sec. 128(5)). PCPNDT: 2 years. Clinical trial records: 5 years after the trial. CERT-In logs: 180 days. Destroy records only under a Board-approved retention policy, and never anything under a litigation hold.

## 12. Recent changes the tool is told about

- BNS, BNSS and BSA replaced the IPC, CrPC and Evidence Act from 1 July 2024.
- The Income-tax Act 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, and section numbers changed.
- The Labour Codes came into force on 21 November 2025.
- The DPDP Rules 2025 are being phased in.
- DIR-3 KYC moved to a once-in-three-years cycle under the 2025 amendment to Rule 12A. The calendar asks the user to confirm this on the MCA portal.

> **A note on accuracy.** Dates, thresholds and forms change often. The calendar, checklists and the assistant's answers flag points to confirm on the official portal. Important matters should still be reviewed by the CS and, where needed, outside counsel.
